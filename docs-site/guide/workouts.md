# Workouts and fitness

The watch can record your own workout while you umpire: heart rate, steps, distance and calories, from the first period to the final whistle.

It's off until you turn it on, and it's yours: it never goes into the match or onto the website.

## Turning it on

- **Wear OS:** on the watch, **Settings → Record workout**. The watch asks to use your heart rate and physical activity; allow both. If you refuse, it stays off.
- **Apple Watch:** on the watch, **Settings → Record workout**, and allow Health access.

Distance is worked out from your steps: GPS isn't used, to save battery.

?> On Wear OS, only one app can record a workout at a time. If you've started a workout in Samsung Health, the match isn't recorded (your Samsung Health workout carries on).

## Seeing it

When the match reaches your phone, its page shows **Your workout**: a heart rate chart through the match, the time, distance, steps, average and highest heart rate, and calories.

## Samsung Health and Health Connect (Android)

The phone can save each workout to **Health Connect**, Android's shared store for health data, where Samsung Health, Google Fit's successor and other fitness apps pick it up.

- **One at a time:** on the match page, under Your workout, tap **Save to Health Connect**.
- **Every match:** **Settings → Health Connect → Save workouts automatically**.

The first time, Android asks what FH Match Centre may save; allow them all. The app only ever writes to Health Connect; it never reads anything from it.

For Samsung Health to show them, turn on its sync with Health Connect: in Samsung Health, **Settings → Health Connect**.

The workout appears as an "Other workout" named after the match ("Umpiring: Hawks M1 v Reading M1"). Saving the same match again replaces it rather than adding a second one.

## Apple Health

On an Apple Watch, with Record workout on, each match is saved as a **Hockey** workout in the Health app, and counts towards your Activity rings.
